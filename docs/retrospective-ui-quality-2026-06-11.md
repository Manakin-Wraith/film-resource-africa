# Film Resource Africa — Site Quality Retrospective

**Date:** 11 June 2026
**Audience:** Stakeholders & partners
**Status:** Completed and live on film-resource-africa.com

---

## Why we did this

Film Resource Africa has grown quickly — new sections, new features, new pages — and that speed came with a cost. The site had been built piece by piece, and small inconsistencies had crept in everywhere: buttons that looked slightly different from page to page, text that was harder to read than it should be, pop-up windows that behaved differently depending on where you opened them, and parts of the site that couldn't be used at all without a mouse.

None of these were dramatic on their own. Together, they made the site feel less polished than the work it showcases, and they were quietly making every future improvement slower and more expensive to build.

So we paused, took stock, and fixed it properly.

## What we did — in three steps

**1. We audited everything.**
We did a top-to-bottom review of every page and every screen and produced a detailed list of **163 individual design issues**, graded by how serious each one was — from "this affects whether people can use the site" down to "this is a one-pixel inconsistency."

**2. We fixed it, methodically.**
Rather than patching things one by one, we worked in carefully planned stages over a single focused effort:

- **We made the site fully usable by keyboard.** Anyone navigating without a mouse — including people using assistive technology — can now see exactly where they are on the page and reach every menu, button, and form. This was the single biggest accessibility gap, and it's closed.
- **We made the text easier to read.** Faded, low-contrast text across the site was brought up to recognised readability standards, and the smallest text sizes were raised so labels and details are legible on every screen.
- **We made everything behave consistently.** All pop-up windows now open, close, and scroll the same way (including closing with the Escape key, and no longer letting the page scroll behind them). Cards, buttons, search bars, and filters now share one consistent design across the whole site.
- **We built reusable building blocks.** Instead of 50+ pages each carrying their own slightly different version of a button or a card, the site now draws from a single shared kit. This is invisible to visitors, but it means future changes — even a full visual refresh — can be made once and appear everywhere, rather than being rebuilt page by page.
- **We fixed live bugs along the way**, including pop-ups that let the page scroll underneath them and a mobile layout problem that hid the "Member login" button behind the bottom navigation bar (spotted by the team on a real phone — exactly the kind of feedback that helps).

**3. We checked our own work.**
After finishing, we ran a second independent audit against the original list of 163 issues. That re-check confirmed the fixes, caught nine small items we had missed (fixed the same day), and even found six issues in the original list that turned out not to be real problems.

## Where things stand

| | |
|---|---|
| Issues identified | 163 |
| Fixed and live | 105 |
| Deliberately kept as-is (intentional design choices, verified) | ~30 |
| Scheduled for the next phase (the members' area) | 22 |
| Original audit findings that proved incorrect | 6 |

**Every serious and moderate issue on the public site is now resolved.** What remains is a planned second phase: applying the same treatment to the logged-in members' area, which we intentionally set aside so the public-facing site — the part our audience and partners see — came first.

## What this means for you

- **Visitors get a more credible site.** The polish now matches the ambition. First impressions for filmmakers, funders, and partners are stronger.
- **The site is more inclusive.** People using keyboards or assistive technology can use everything. This matters to our community and to institutional partners who expect accessibility.
- **Mobile works properly.** The majority of our audience visits on a phone; the mobile experience got the most attention.
- **Future changes are faster and cheaper.** Because the site now runs on shared building blocks, a future rebrand or redesign becomes a matter of adjusting the foundation once — not reworking dozens of pages. We estimated this would have taken weeks before; it's now days.

## What's next

1. **Members' area refresh** — bringing the logged-in member experience (profiles, directory, project tools) up to the same standard, using the building blocks created in this effort.
2. **A small list of polish items** carried forward deliberately — none affect usability.
3. Everything above is documented internally, so this standard is maintained as the site grows.
