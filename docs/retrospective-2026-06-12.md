## The short version

Over the past six weeks, Film Resource Africa moved from "a directory with a newsletter" to a working media-and-services platform: the opportunity engine now verifies and tracks funding calls largely on its own, members have their own premium briefing, the public site was overhauled top to bottom for quality and credibility, and the editorial voice is reaching the industry through news coverage and social channels.

The next phase shifts from building the audience layer to monetising it — starting with the first finance-facing tools and the first hand-matched introduction between a verified project and a funder.

---

## What we accomplished

### 1. The opportunity engine got smarter — and more trustworthy

The heart of FRA is the promise that every funding call, lab, and grant listed is **real, current, and accurately described**. We invested heavily here:

- **Every opportunity is now verified before it goes live**, with safeguards that stop the system from ever "inventing" details that aren't in the original source. If a deadline or eligibility rule can't be confirmed, the listing doesn't publish.
- **The system now remembers funding cycles.** Major programmes (over 100 so far) have their application history on record, so when a fund reopens — as the NFVF's new production and development call just did — we catch it and re-list it quickly rather than waiting for someone to notice.
- **Stale and expired listings are retired automatically**, so visitors never plan around a dead deadline.
- **News coverage is filtered for African relevance** and articles that turn out to be paywalled or cut off are caught before subscribers ever see them.

**Why it matters:** trust is our entire moat. A filmmaker who gets burned once by a phantom deadline doesn't come back. This work is invisible when it's working — which is the point.

### 2. Each opportunity now has its own page

Opportunities used to open in a pop-up window. Each one now has its own dedicated, shareable web page. This sounds small; it isn't:

- Filmmakers can **share a specific opportunity** on WhatsApp, social media, or by email — and that link works forever.
- **Search engines can now find and rank our opportunity pages**, which turns every listing into a doorway to the site.
- The site navigation was simplified to five clear sections, so first-time visitors find what they need faster.

### 3. Members got their own premium experience

- A new **Members Brief** — a members-only opportunities digest with a distinct premium look — was designed, tested, and sent to paying members for the first time on 10 June. It ranks every live opportunity by deadline urgency, so members see what needs action first.
- Member onboarding, profiles, logos, and project tools all received fixes and polish, and members can now create and update their own project entries end-to-end.
- The membership offer was reinforced in campaigns with a concrete benefit: where an opportunity is open to organisations only, **FRA can co-submit on behalf of individual members** — a real, tangible reason to join.

### 4. The newsletter became a campaign machine

- The weekly digest now carries a **featured-opportunity spotlight**, an industry-directory section, and partner placements — and every edition can be safely proofed before sending.
- We ran our first **rapid-response campaign** (the ITC mentorship deadline extension): a standalone alert to 270 subscribers, a featured slot in the weekly digest, and a LinkedIn post, all in one day. The playbook is now reusable for any urgent opportunity.
- Midweek news editions, last-minute deadline alerts, and community spotlights all run from the same reliable system.

### 5. The public site was overhauled for quality

Covered fully in the [11 June retrospective](retrospective-ui-quality-2026-06-11.md), in brief: a 163-point audit of the entire site, every serious and moderate issue fixed and verified live — full keyboard and assistive-technology access, readable text everywhere, consistent design across all pages, and a rebuilt foundation that makes any future redesign dramatically cheaper. The polish now matches the ambition.

### 6. Editorial presence

FRA is publishing industry news with its own voice — most recently covering Makgano Mamabolo and Yemi Oyefuwa's selection for the European Showrunner Training 2026 and the Adiaha Award — and amplifying it on LinkedIn. This is the trust-and-audience flywheel: coverage brings filmmakers, filmmakers bring listings, listings bring funders.

## Next steps — in plain terms

The platform's long-term thesis is: **FRA builds the audience and the trust; a paid finance layer (AFX) turns verified projects into deal flow for funders.** The next 30–90 days start that transition.

### Next 30 days

1. **Ship the Rebate Calculator.** A free public tool where any producer can estimate what South Africa's film incentives are actually worth to their production, and receive the result as a document. It is the first finance-flavoured tool on the site, it is highly shareable, and it captures exactly the audience the finance layer needs. *One open item: confirming the current government incentive rates before launch.*
2. **Open the Finance-Ready intake.** A simple application where producers submit the documents that make a project fundable (script sample, CVs, company registration, budget top-sheet, proof of own investment). Verification is done by hand at this stage — deliberately.
3. **Make the first match.** Personally introduce at least one verified project to one of the South African studios already enquiring. One closed introduction proves the model before we automate anything.
4. **Members' area refresh.** Bring the logged-in member experience up to the same standard as the newly polished public site.

### Next 90 days

5. **Self-serve project workspace.** Producers build and maintain their project package on the site itself, with a verified badge once an auditor signs off.
6. **First paying studio subscribers.** Funders and studios pay for access to verified, finance-ready deal flow.
7. **Editorial that feeds the funnel.** Plain-English briefs on what the top African funds actually want, and a glossary of film-finance terms with African context.

### How we'll measure success

Not subscribers, not page views. The north-star metric is **production financing won by FRA-registered projects** — funded output, reported publicly once a year.
