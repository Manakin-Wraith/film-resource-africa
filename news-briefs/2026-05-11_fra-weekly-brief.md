## Top items this week

### 1. AMVCA 12 — "My Father's Shadow" sweeps Africa's biggest screen awards (9–10 May, Lagos)

The 12th Africa Magic Viewers' Choice Awards was held Saturday 9 May at Eko Hotel & Suites, Lagos, and is the most-discussed African-industry story on LinkedIn this week. Headline outcomes:

- **My Father's Shadow** (dir. Akinola Davies Jr.) won at least five categories including **Best Movie**, **Best Director**, **Best Cinematography**, **Best Music Score** and **Best Writing**. It is the same film that made history in May 2025 as the first Nigerian title in the Cannes Un Certain Regard official selection.
- **Linda Ejiofor-Suleiman** made history with a **double win** — Best Lead Actress and Best Supporting Actress in the same ceremony.
- **Uzor Arukwe** — Best Lead Actor.
- **Bucci Franklin** — Best Supporting Actor (*To Kill a Monkey*).
- **Lateef Adedimeji** — Best Indigenous Film.
- **Industry Merit / lifetime recognition**: Sola Sobowale and Kanayo O. Kanayo.

Why it matters for FRA: AMVCA is increasingly cited on LinkedIn (Muoma Obinna, JohnMac Clement, others) as the de facto pan-African benchmark — and MultiChoice's training (MTF), distribution (Showmax) and awards stack is being framed as a model for sustained corporate investment in continental cinema.

### 2. FAME Week Africa 2026 opens registration — France is "Destination Focus"

Multiple LinkedIn posts this week (DAWN Disabled Audio-Visual Workers' Network, THISABILITY Newspaper, others) confirm that **registration is now open for FAME Week Africa 2026**, the pan-African creative-industries marketplace.

- **Dates:** 28 October – 1 November 2026
- **Venue:** Cape Town (hosted by the City of Cape Town, CTICC)
- **Four flagship platforms under one roof:** MIP Africa (B2B film/TV/digital market), Muziki Africa, African Fashion Forum, FAME Shorts Film Festival.
- **Destination Focus: France** — flagged by French Ambassador David Martinon as a formal strengthening of Franco-African creative-industry ties. Portfolio Director Martin Hiller positioned FAME as a "critical meeting point" for global engagement with African content.

Implication for FRA: this is the highest-leverage pan-African market event still ahead in 2026. Worth surfacing to FRA users as a deadline tracker (pre-register window) and a partnership/visa entry point given the France Destination Focus.

Sources: [Bizcommunity — Registration opens](https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/fame-week-africa-2026-opens-registration-514060a) · [MediaUpdate — FAME Week Africa 2026](https://www.mediaupdate.co.za/media/161106/fame-week-africa-2026-opens-registration) · [Official site](https://www.fameweekafrica.com/)

### 3. British Council launches Film Lab Africa 2 — Lagos, 5 May 2026

Phase two of the British Council's flagship film accelerator launched in Lagos on 5 May, delivered in partnership with **EbonyLife Creative Academy** and **Iconic Steps**.

- **New elements vs. first cohort:** dedicated writers' lab, stronger focus on episodic storytelling and TV pilot development.
- **Eligibility:** emerging writers, producers and directors aged 18–35.
- **Pipeline:** online training in episodic writing → physical writers' residency → script development → pilot production funding → industry showcase events with investors and distributors.
- **Track record:** the first edition (2023) attracted 600+ applications, with 60 selected.

Implication for FRA: this is a concrete, time-bound talent-development opportunity to surface in the FRA opportunities directory. Episodic writing is the gap most cited by African producers chasing streamer commissions.

Sources: [Vanguard — Second cohort launched](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2026/05/british-council-launches-second-cohort-of-film-lab-africa-in-nigeria/) · [BellaNaija — Film Lab Africa 2](https://www.bellanaija.com/2026/05/british-council-launches-filmlab-africa2/) · [Premium Times — Boost Nigerian storytelling talent](https://www.premiumtimesng.com/news/more-news/877332-british-council-launches-film-lab-africa-2-to-boost-nigerian-film-tv-storytelling-talent.html)

### 4. AISFM 2026 — Africa's first dedicated Short Film Market wraps inaugural edition

LinkedIn posts from organiser Gbenga Adeoti (The Filmjoint) and the AISFM account confirm the **inaugural African International Short Film Market** has just concluded in Lagos.

- **Co-founders:** Gbenga Adeoti (Nigeria, The Filmjoint), with Kagabo Nkubiri and Theodore Ishimwe (Rwanda).
- **Format:** 3-day market — workshops, pitch sessions and networking at CC Hub; final-day screenings at EbonyLife Cinema.
- **Slate:** 18 curated pitch projects from across the continent + a project pool of ~40 selected films.
- **Mandate:** address the structural gap between short-film production volume and distribution/monetisation pathways.

Implication for FRA: a brand-new continental market node specifically for short-form. Worth tagging filmmakers in the FRA directory whose work qualifies, and tracking AISFM submissions windows for 2027.

Sources: [Sinema Focus — Adeoti makes the case for Africa's first short film market](https://www.sinemafocus.com/africa-first-short-film-market-aisfm/) · [What Kept Me Up — AISFM first edition in Lagos](https://whatkeptmeup.com/nigerian-film-news/new-african-short-film-market-aisfm-to-hold-first-edition-in-lagos/) · [AISFM official site](https://aisfm.org/explore)

## Context worth keeping on FRA's radar

**South African rebate crisis still unresolved.** Not a fresh-this-week item, but a recurring theme in pan-African discourse. The DTIC's Film and TV Production Incentive panel last met in March 2024; no projects have been approved in the 2024/25 or 2025/26 financial years, prompting a January 2026 march to Parliament in Cape Town. This continues to depress confidence in South Africa as a service-production destination, with knock-on effects for pan-African co-productions. ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-02-17/south-africa-s-film-industry-founders-as-state-withholds-support) · [Akoroko — Rebate Policy Patchwork](https://akoroko.com/rebate-policy-patchwork-africas-uneven-landscape/))

**MultiChoice Talent Factory 2026 applications open.** Pan-African nine-month hybrid training programme — academies in Nigeria, Kenya and Zambia, serving 14 African countries; 296 alumni since 2018, 42+ training films broadcast on DStv/GOtv/Showmax. ([MSME Africa](https://msmeafricaonline.com/multichoice-talent-factory-opens-applications-for-2026-film-and-tv-training-empowers-african-creatives/))

**AACC still not operational.** The African Audiovisual and Cinema Commission, adopted by the AU in 2019, still requires ratification by 15 member states to become operational — a structural policy gap that limits pan-African coordination. ([African Union](https://au.int/en/african-audio-visual-cinema-commission-aacc))
