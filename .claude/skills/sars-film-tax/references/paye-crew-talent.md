# PAYE, Crew, and Talent — Reference

The starting point for any crew or talent payment is the SARS *Guide on the Employers' Tax Responsibilities with respect to Artists, Models or Crew in the Film Industry* (LAPD-IT-G05). The Guide is the published SARS view and should be cited by section in any deliverable.

## The three characterisations

Every individual paid by an SPV falls into one of three boxes:

1. **Employee** — fully PAYE, SDL, UIF, COIDA. IRP5 at year-end.
2. **Independent contractor (true freelancer)** — no PAYE; the contractor is a provisional taxpayer. SPV issues no IRP5, but may need to issue an IT3(a) if certain reportable thresholds are met.
3. **"Deemed employee" / labour broker / personal service provider** — PAYE applies despite the freelancer label, because the statutory tests in the **Fourth Schedule** to the Income Tax Act are failed.

The Fourth Schedule tests (paragraph 1 definition of "employee", read with the "independent trader" exclusion) turn on the substance of the engagement, not the contract label:

- Does the SPV control the manner in which services are rendered?
- Are the services rendered on the SPV's premises (set) and at hours the SPV directs?
- Is the worker integrated into the SPV's organisation?
- Does the worker bear no real entrepreneurial risk?
- Does the worker supply their own tools and equipment? (Materially relevant for camera/sound/grip teams who own kit.)

Heads of department who own their kit, invoice through their own companies, take simultaneous gigs, and bear schedule risk are typically independent contractors. Day-rate runners, PAs, and AD-pool crew typically fail the tests and must be PAYE'd.

## Artists, models, and on-camera talent

The SARS film-industry guide treats on-camera talent specifically. Key points:

- A **resident artist** paid for a performance is generally an employee for PAYE purposes unless they unambiguously satisfy the independent-contractor tests. Loan-out companies do not automatically convert PAYE — the personal service provider rules (Fourth Schedule, paragraph 1, "personal service provider" definition) can pull PAYE back through the company at the **27% CIT rate as a minimum PAYE rate**.
- A **non-resident artist or entertainer** is taxed under the dedicated regime in **ss47A–K** — see below.

## Foreign artists and entertainers — ss47A–K

A non-resident who performs in SA as an entertainer or sportsperson is subject to a **15% final withholding tax** on amounts received in respect of that performance. The SPV is the withholding agent and must:

- Withhold 15% from the gross payment.
- Pay it to SARS by the end of the month following the month of payment.
- Submit a return on the prescribed form.

DTA relief may reduce or remove the withholding only where the relevant DTA explicitly addresses entertainers/sportspersons (most do, often denying treaty relief specifically for performance income — check the article in each treaty).

## SDL and UIF

- **SDL**: 1% of leviable salaries, payable monthly via EMP201. Exempt if total annual payroll ≤ R500,000.
- **UIF**: 1% employer + 1% employee (capped at the prescribed ceiling), via EMP201.
- Both apply to **employees**, not to true independent contractors.

## EMP201, EMP501, IRP5

- Monthly EMP201 (PAYE/SDL/UIF) by the 7th of the following month.
- Bi-annual EMP501 reconciliation (interim by end of October, final by end of May).
- IRP5 / IT3(a) issued to each employee/contractor in the May reconciliation.

## Production-specific complications

1. **Multi-territory shoots**: Crew flown to SA for an SA shoot whose home tax residence is elsewhere may trigger SA PAYE under the Fourth Schedule unless DTA short-stay provisions are satisfied (typically 183 days). Check the relevant DTA *and* the source rules (s9(2)(h)).
2. **Equity participation / deferred fees**: Talent paid partly in points or deferred fees creates timing issues — PAYE generally on accrual, not on cash. Document the accrual moment.
3. **Travel and subsistence allowances**: Use the published SARS table for daily subsistence (overnight, in-republic, out-of-republic). Amounts above the published rate are taxable.
4. **Per diems**: If not against the subsistence table and properly documented, default to taxable.
