# VAT for Film Production SPVs — Reference

VAT is, in the current incentive vacuum, **the single most material live tax-planning lever** for SA productions servicing foreign principals. Treat the rules below carefully.

## Registration thresholds

- **Compulsory**: taxable supplies exceeding **R1 million** in any consecutive 12-month period (or written contract that will exceed R1m in the next 12 months). s23(1) VAT Act.
- **Voluntary**: from **R50,000** taxable supplies in the prior 12 months. s23(3).
- For an SPV, voluntary registration is almost always advisable from day one, because:
  - Input VAT on pre-production (development, scouting, legal, equipment hire) becomes claimable.
  - Foreign-funded productions will likely be supplying zero-rated services and will run **persistent input-VAT credit positions** that they want to refund regularly.

## Standard rate

- **15%** on taxable supplies in SA (s7(1)(a)).
- Output VAT on local distribution, local broadcast licence fees, local sponsorship, ancillary merchandise.

## Zero-rating of exported services — the core planning lever

Under **s11(2)(l)** of the VAT Act, services supplied to a non-resident who is **not in SA at the time the services are rendered** are zero-rated, **provided** the services are not supplied directly in connection with land or movable property situated in SA at that time (s11(2)(l)(i)–(iii) exclusions).

The SARS view on "production services" supplied by an SA SPV to a foreign producer is nuanced. The historically-accepted analysis (subject to facts):

- The **output** of the production services — the audiovisual work or its master — is delivered to a non-resident principal.
- The non-resident is the contracting customer and is not present in SA when the services are rendered.
- The services are not "directly in connection with movable property situated in SA" in the s11(2)(l)(ii) sense, because the relevant "property" is the IP / master being created, which has no fixed situs.

If those facts hold, the SA SPV invoices the foreign producer at **0% VAT** and recovers input VAT on local production spend in full. This is the closest live equivalent to a "rebate" for foreign productions, but it is **not** an incentive — it is the normal operation of the VAT Act.

### Required to support zero-rating (documentary proof per Interpretation Note 31 and Binding General Ruling 11):
- Written contract with the foreign principal naming a non-SA address.
- Proof the principal is not in SA when services are rendered.
- Evidence of receipt of payment in foreign currency through the SA banking system (or other approved means).
- Tax invoice marked "Zero-rated supply — s11(2)(l)".

**Risk:** SARS has, in some audits, recharacterised "production services" as connected to either personnel or equipment situated in SA at the time. Hold the documentary file from day one.

## Input VAT — production-specific points

- Entertainment (s17(2)(a)) — generally **no input VAT** on food, drink, accommodation supplied for entertainment. Catering on a working set is typically allowed; per-diem-equivalent meals to talent are often denied. Document the production-purpose intent on every invoice.
- Motor cars (s17(2)(c)) — no input VAT on a "motor car" as defined; light delivery vehicles, trucks, and most production vehicles fall outside the denial.
- Imported equipment and services (s7(1)(b)/(c), s14) — reverse-charge VAT on imported services where the recipient does not use them ≥95% in making taxable supplies. Mostly not an issue for productions making 100% taxable supplies.

## VAT cycle

- Category A or B: bi-monthly returns (VAT201).
- Category C: monthly (mandatory above R30m turnover).
- Refund SPVs typically apply for **Category C monthly** to accelerate refunds. Worth requesting at registration.
- Refunds: SARS may verify before paying. Build verification delay into cashflow assumptions — 21 business days is the statutory window but practical delays are common.

## Common SPV VAT errors

1. Registering only when compulsory threshold is hit, losing input VAT on development.
2. Zero-rating without the BGR 11 documentary set.
3. Treating crew per diems and entertainment as fully input-claimable.
4. Failing to account for VAT on barter / contra deals (location, kit, talent equity).
5. Not registering the production bank account as the SPV's primary banking — payments must flow through the SPV's own account.
