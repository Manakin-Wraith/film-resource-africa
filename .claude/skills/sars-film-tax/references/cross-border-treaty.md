# Cross-Border & Treaty — Reference

Cross-border tax matters for SA-based film SPVs cluster around four flows:

1. **Inbound service fees** (foreign producer pays SA SPV) — see VAT zero-rating in `vat-film-production.md`. Income-tax side: ordinary SA-sourced trading income, taxable in the SPV at 27% CIT.
2. **Outbound royalties** (SA SPV or HoldCo licenses IP to a foreign distributor) — royalties received from foreign sources are taxable in SA on residents; royalties paid from SA to non-residents trigger **s49A–D withholding at 15%**, reducible by DTA (commonly to 0–10%).
3. **Outbound dividends** (SA SPV distributes to foreign shareholders) — **20%** dividends WHT, reducible by DTA (commonly 5–15% depending on ownership threshold).
4. **Foreign artists / entertainers** — **ss47A–K**, 15% final WHT. See `paye-crew-talent.md`. DTA relief is narrower than for ordinary business profits — many DTAs explicitly preserve source-state taxation of entertainers.

## DTA framework

SA has DTAs in force with most relevant production-finance jurisdictions. For each transaction, do **four checks**:

1. **Is the counterparty a tax resident of the treaty state?** Demand a current Tax Residency Certificate (TRC).
2. **Which DTA article applies?** Business profits (art. 7), royalties (art. 12), dividends (art. 10), entertainers/sportspersons (art. 17). Do not collapse royalties into business profits — the WHT is article-specific.
3. **Is there a Limitation on Benefits (LoB) or Principal Purpose Test (PPT)?** Most SA DTAs renegotiated post-2017 include a PPT (MLI). The producer must be able to show a genuine business purpose for the structure.
4. **Is the SA withholding agent satisfied of treaty entitlement before withholding at the reduced rate?** If not, withhold at the full rate and let the foreign party claim a refund; do not under-withhold on assumptions.

## Permanent establishment risk

A foreign producer running a production in SA risks creating a **permanent establishment (PE)** through:

- A fixed place of business (production office leased in the foreign producer's own name).
- A dependent agent habitually concluding contracts in SA for the foreign producer.
- Duration thresholds for service PEs in some DTAs (typically 183 days).

The SPV structure is the canonical PE-avoidance mechanism: the foreign producer contracts with an SA SPV, the SPV is the SA taxpayer, and the foreign producer remains offshore. This works only if the SPV is genuinely the contracting party and is not a bare nominee.

## Transfer pricing — s31

Any cross-border transaction between connected persons (foreign parent ↔ SA SPV; SA SPV ↔ offshore IP HoldCo) must be at arm's length. Production service fees billed by an SA SPV to a foreign parent should be set with a **cost-plus mark-up** that survives a benchmarking study. A pure cost-recovery model (0% margin) will be challenged.

Documentation thresholds: master file + local file required where consolidated group revenue exceeds the prescribed threshold; transactional documentation required for all material related-party transactions regardless.

## Exchange control (SARB FinSurv)

Not tax, but adjacent. Inbound foreign loans, equity, and IP transfers to or from SA require SARB approval or authorised-dealer reporting. Material to:

- Foreign-financed productions taking equity through an SA SPV.
- IP held offshore that was developed (even partly) in SA — emigration of IP is restricted.

Flag exchange control implications in any cross-border structuring memo; defer the actual approval pathway to a SARB-authorised dealer or exchange-control practitioner.
